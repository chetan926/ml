### 2.3 Ground-Truth Label Mapping & Conceptual Boundary
Crowd-worker annotations in SNLI are mapped to a binary classification task:
$$\text{is\_plagiarized} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if original SNLI label is } 0 \text{ (entailment)} \\ 0 & \text{if original SNLI label is } 1 \text{ (neutral) or } 2 \text{ (contradiction)} \end{cases}$$
Ambiguous records (`label = -1`, lack of crowd consensus) were purged during preprocessing.

**Methodological Warning:**
Logical entailment represents semantic truth-conditional inclusion, not dishonest academic text reuse. In this work, the benchmark is strictly used as an adversarial testbed for **Sentence-Level Semantic Equivalence & Text Reuse**. It measures whether models can detect semantic equivalence in the absence of lexical overlap, but does not simulate multi-page thesis plagiarism or citation manipulation.

### 2.4 The Zero-Overlap Phenomenon
Forensic evaluation revealed that **10.57% of positive entailment pairs (109 / 1,031 validation positives) exhibit a TF-IDF cosine similarity of exactly 0.0000**.
- *Example:*

### 3.2 Pair-Generation Protocol & Ground-Truth Labeling
For each assignment task:
1. `original/`: The base reference implementation.
2. `plagiarized/`: Transformed variants generated across 6 Faidhi-Robinson obfuscation levels (`L1` to `L6`):
   - L1: Comments and formatting layout alterations.
   - L2: Systematic identifier, variable, and method renaming.
   - L3: Statement and declaration reordering.
   - L4: Control-flow loop transformation (`for` $\leftrightarrow$ `while`, branch logic swap).
   - L5: Method abstraction and procedure inlining.
   - L6: Compound multi-technique camouflage.
3. `non-plagiarized/`: Genuine independent student submissions solving the same programming assignment.

**Pair Construction Protocol:**
- **Positive Plagiarized Pairs ($Y = 1$):**
  $$(D_{\text{original}}, D_{\text{plagiarized}}^{(L1..L6)}) \implies 355 \text{ total positive pairs.}$$
- **Negative Non-Plagiarized Pairs ($Y = 0$):**
  $$(D_{\text{original}}, D_{\text{independent}}) \quad \text{and} \quad (D_{\text{independent}, A}, D_{\text{independent}, B}) \implies 378 \text{ total negative pairs.}$$
